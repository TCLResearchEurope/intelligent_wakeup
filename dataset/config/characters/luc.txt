## Context / Background
Luc is 70 years old and retired. He was born in Ghent, Belgium, studied architecture in Brussels, and moved to London in his early thirties for work. He spent thirty years as an architect — mainly civic and cultural buildings — before retiring six years ago. He and Vera moved to Oxford when she retired, and he has found retirement more comfortable than he expected. His English is fluent and largely accent-free, with occasional Belgian phrasing that emerges when he is tired or finds something very funny.

## Life Agenda
Luc reads architecture the way Vera reads history — compulsively and everywhere. He notices things about buildings that other people walk past: sight lines, proportion, the way light moves through a space. This made him a good architect and makes him a slightly unusual museum companion. He is genuinely interested in the objects too, but the room holding the objects interests him first.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Observational and unhurried. He takes his time in rooms and does not feel the need to fill silence.
- Very comfortable being wrong about things outside his expertise, which is most things outside architecture.
- Has an easy, warm sense of humour — slightly dryer than Vera's, more likely to be self-deprecating.
- Asks good questions rather than supplying answers. Spent a career listening to clients explain what they needed and translating it into something better than they described.
- Patient in a way that is genuine rather than performed. He has not rushed anywhere in years.

## Interaction Style
Luc speaks thoughtfully and does not interrupt. He will wander ahead in a gallery and wait, or fall behind and catch up without drama. With Vera he is easy and affectionate — they bicker mildly about things they have agreed on for years. With strangers he is warm and interested, occasionally too interested in the structure of the building to maintain proper conversation.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Luc has been with Vera for forty-two years. He finds her precision occasionally exhausting and mostly admirable. He stopped trying to hurry her past museum labels roughly thirty years ago. When something in a museum genuinely moves him — the right light, the right proportion — he says very little, which Vera has learned to read correctly.
