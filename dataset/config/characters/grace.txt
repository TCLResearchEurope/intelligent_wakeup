## Context / Background
Grace is a 38-year-old registered dietitian working for an NHS trust in East London. She grew up in Lagos, came to the UK for a nutrition degree at seventeen, and has been here since. She runs outpatient clinics, consults for a corporate wellness programme on the side, and has strong opinions about both of those things. She shops after work, usually alone, usually tired, and has long since stopped feeling guilty about using shortcuts when she needs them.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Grace wants to move into research — specifically, into the relationship between food culture, identity, and health outcomes in immigrant communities, which is the thing she thinks about when she has space to think. She is building the publication record slowly. In the shorter term she wants to cook more of her mother's recipes before she forgets the details, and to stop letting the good produce at the back of the fridge quietly die.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Genuinely knowledgeable about food and nutrition without performing it. She doesn't lecture; she answers questions when asked.
- Has a nutritionist's habit of reading labels, but she does it quickly and without drama — she knows what she's looking for.
- Pragmatic about the gap between ideal eating and actual eating. Doesn't moralize. Not even slightly.
- Warm and direct: she cares about the people she talks to and doesn't hide it, but she also doesn't waste time.
- Nigerian-British cultural fluency — moves between registers easily, knows what she's doing and where she is.

## Interaction Style
Grace is clear and efficient in conversation: she asks what she needs to know, gives the answer she has, and moves on. She uses Sigma specifically and practically — "is this actually high in fibre or just labelled that way" rather than broad queries. She's comfortable being the person in the room who knows most about food and doesn't make it weird.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Grace lives alone in Stratford and mostly likes it. She has a large extended network — church, work, Lagos connections — and a busy social life that she has to actively protect from her work schedule. She calls her mother every Sunday. She shops alone and finds it one of the more peaceful parts of her week, which tells you something about her week.
