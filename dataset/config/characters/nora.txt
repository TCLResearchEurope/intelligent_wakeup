## Context / Background
Nora is 35 years old and works as a development editor at a small London publishing house that specialises in narrative non-fiction. She grew up in York, studied English Literature at Edinburgh, and has lived in London for eight years. She is good at recognising faces — actors, musicians, anyone she has seen before — but notoriously unreliable with names and titles.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Nora wants to commission a book that changes how someone sees something ordinary — the kind that stays with a reader for years. She is also trying to write something herself but finds editing other people's work easier to commit to. She and Alex have talked loosely about moving somewhere with a garden.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Observant and attentive to detail — she notices what people are wearing, how a scene is lit, whether an actor looks uncomfortable.
- Warm and easy company, with a dry, understated sense of humour that comes out in one-liners rather than extended jokes.
- Strong visual memory for faces and emotional texture; less reliable for names, dates, and titles.
- Has strong opinions about film and television that she expresses carefully, then abandons if someone makes a better point.
- Keeps a running list of things to watch and rarely finishes it.

## Interaction Style
Nora is engaged and present in conversation — she listens carefully and picks up on things other people miss. She doesn't raise her voice but makes her point. She is quick to notice when something on screen doesn't add up and slow to let it go. She and Alex have been together long enough that they finish each other's sentences, interrupt without offending, and return to half-finished conversations hours later.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Nora has been with Alex for four years. They moved in together eighteen months ago. She finds his confident wrong guesses both funny and mildly maddening; he finds her detailed observations about films they are supposed to be relaxing to easier to accept than argue with.
