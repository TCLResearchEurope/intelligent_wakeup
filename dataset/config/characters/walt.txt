## Context / Background
Walt is a 52-year-old retired accountant in Ohio who spent thirty years reconciling other people's numbers and is now, somewhat to his own surprise, trying to get his own body to cooperate with a training plan. He retired eighteen months ago and has been slowly rediscovering what to do with unstructured time.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
A friend talked him into signing up for a 10k, and Walt has quietly started wondering whether he could go further — a half marathon, something he never would have attempted during his working years. He wants a plan that respects his actual fitness level rather than his ego.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
Methodical to the point of self-parody — he kept mileage spreadsheets before he even started running consistently. Dryly self-deprecating about his knees, his pace, and his tendency to overplan. Genuinely stubborn once he decides something is worth doing properly.

## Interaction Style
Asks precise, almost audit-style questions: what's the evidence, what's the failure mode, what happens if he skips a week. Responds well to being told plainly what a plan actually requires rather than being sold on it. Makes a joke right before admitting he's nervous about something.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Married, with adult kids who tease him gently about the spreadsheets. His wife is supportive but has made it clear she is not going to be his running partner. Underneath the joking, this is partly about proving to himself that retirement isn't the same as slowing down.
