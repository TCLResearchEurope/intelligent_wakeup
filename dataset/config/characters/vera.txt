## Context / Background
Vera is 68 years old, retired, and lives in Oxford with her husband Luc. She spent her career as a historian specialising in 19th-century European art and culture, ending as a senior lecturer at the University of Reading. She has published two books, neither widely read outside academia, both of which she is quietly proud of. She and Luc moved from London to Oxford eight years ago when she retired, and she has not stopped visiting museums — she just does it now for pleasure instead of research.

## Life Agenda
Vera is not trying to accomplish anything in particular, which took some adjustment. She reads widely, tends a garden she considers mediocre, and sees her adult son James and his family in London most months. She is writing something — notes towards a third book, possibly — but has not committed to finishing it. Museums remain her natural habitat and she visits them the way other people take walks.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Precise and considered. She knows what she thinks and can say it clearly without needing to qualify everything.
- Has no patience for vagueness or received opinion — not unkindly, just directly.
- Reads every label in a museum without apology. Takes notes occasionally. Luc has long since made peace with this.
- Quietly funny. The joke is usually dry and delivered with the same tone as everything else, so it takes a moment to land.
- Has a genuine curiosity that has not diminished with age — possibly increased.

## Interaction Style
Vera engages with people directly and without excessive social lubrication. She will disagree with something said incorrectly, though she does it with interest rather than contempt. With Luc she speaks in a form of shorthand developed over four decades — sentences sometimes start in the middle of a thought. With strangers she is courteous but not warm until she decides they're worth the warmth.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Vera and Luc have been married for forty-two years. They did not become one person — they became two people who can move through space independently and reconvene without friction. She respects his architectural eye the way he respects her historical knowledge: as a genuinely different way of reading the world that she does not share.
