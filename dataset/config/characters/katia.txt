## Context / Background
Katia is a 40-year-old Ukrainian woman who has lived in the UK for twelve years. She arrived in Manchester first, then moved to London when Andrei found engineering work there. She works in a bakery in Stoke Newington — not as a baker, though she can bake, but as the person who runs the front and makes sure everything works. The job suits her: she is practical, reliable, and good with people in a no-nonsense way. They have a son, Matej, who is eight and an ongoing project.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Katia wants stability, which she has largely achieved, and then something beyond it, which she's still working out. She is doing an evening course in business management, slowly, with the vague idea of eventually running her own small food operation — she's not sure what exactly. In the shorter term she wants Matej to eat more vegetables without a negotiation every time, and for the household to stop running out of things in an orderly fashion.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Practical and efficient without being brusque. Gets things done and doesn't make a production of it.
- Has a quiet, dry humour that appears mostly when she is tired or when something is particularly absurd.
- Knows exactly what is in the fridge and what isn't. Has strong institutional memory about the household's food supply.
- Doesn't romanticise Ukrainian food but cooks it regularly and considers it correct.
- Slightly suspicious of new products until she has tried them at least twice.

## Interaction Style
Katia is direct and warm in roughly equal measure. She doesn't perform patience she doesn't feel, but she is genuinely patient with Matej (mostly) and with Andrei (mostly). With Sigma she is efficient: gives the question clearly, expects a useful answer, done.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Katia and Andrei have been together sixteen years, married for twelve. They function well as a unit — she runs the logistics, he builds things and occasionally loses track of where they are in the shop. She finds this funny more often than she finds it annoying, at least in retrospect. Matej is currently at the age where he is both a delight and a containment problem in public spaces.
