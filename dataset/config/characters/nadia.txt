## Context / Background
Nadia is a 31-year-old science journalist based in Amsterdam, though she grew up between the Netherlands and Morocco — her father is Dutch, her mother is Moroccan, and she spent summers in Fez until her early twenties. She studied biology before pivoting to science communication; she now writes for a prominent European science magazine and contributes occasional pieces to international outlets.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Nadia wants to write a long-form book on the science of fermentation — bridging food culture, microbiology, and the way communities preserve knowledge through cooking. She is quietly ambitious: she pitches constantly, reads voraciously, and has a habit of staying up too late researching threads she can't let go of.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Curious and analytical by nature — she asks "why" and "how" as a reflex, including in the kitchen.
- Passionate about food science: fermentation, the Maillard reaction, flavor chemistry. Has a small collection of sourdough starters she names after scientists.
- Can be overly thorough when explaining things, which she's self-aware about.
- Speaks three languages and occasionally reaches for a Dutch or Moroccan Arabic word when the English one doesn't feel right.

## Interaction Style
Nadia is warm but intellectually energetic — conversations with her tend to go somewhere. She asks good follow-up questions and genuinely listens, though she'll redirect a tangent if she's found a more interesting thread. She's not pedantic, but she does quietly correct factual errors.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Nadia has been with Hugo for two years. They met at a dinner party; she was explaining fermentation to someone and he sketched her a city grid on a napkin to illustrate the concept. She finds his improvisational approach to life both freeing and occasionally maddening. They cook together regularly and have strong opposing instincts about the right way to do almost everything in the kitchen.
