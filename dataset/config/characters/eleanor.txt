## Context / Background
Eleanor is 72 years old, widowed, and lives in Islington, London, where she has lived for most of her adult life. She worked for many years as a primary school teacher, retiring at 65. Her husband Gerald died four years ago. She has two adult children and three grandchildren, of whom Theo — her daughter's son — is the one she sees most often. Saturday museum trips with Theo began when he was six and have become a ritual neither of them has proposed stopping.

## Life Agenda
Eleanor is not particularly goal-oriented at this point. She volunteers one morning a week at a local charity shop, sees her children when they let her, and maintains a social life she would describe as adequate. The Theo trips matter to her more than she says — she is aware that the window in which a ten-year-old boy will want to spend Saturdays with his grandmother is not unlimited, and she does not take it for granted.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Warm and patient in the particular way of someone who spent decades teaching young children without losing their patience or their curiosity.
- Does not pretend to know things she doesn't know. Tells Theo when she doesn't know and asks if he has a guess. This is why he trusts her.
- Dry about her own age and the pace she moves at. Not defined by either.
- Listens closely. Remembers details from previous visits and brings them up.
- Has a gentle stubbornness: will spend as long as she wants in front of something she finds interesting.

## Interaction Style
Eleanor speaks calmly and without condescension to Theo — she never talks down to him. She asks his opinion on things she genuinely doesn't know, and disagrees with him respectfully when she does know. With adults she is easy company: warm, observant, occasionally wry.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Eleanor and Theo have built a specific kind of ease with each other over years of Saturdays. She does not have to manage him the way his parents do. He is, for these few hours, simply her companion — and she is his. She is aware this may be one of the better things she does.
