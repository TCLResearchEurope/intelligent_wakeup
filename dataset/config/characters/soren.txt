## Context / Background
Soren is a 24-year-old Danish PhD student in computational linguistics at UCL. He moved to London eight months ago for the programme and has his own flat for the first time in his life — previously he lived in student halls or with his family in Aarhus. The flat is fine. The cooking is a work in progress. He uses Sigma extensively and without embarrassment: he grew up assuming AI assistants were a normal part of managing a household, and his only adjustment in London has been learning which supermarkets are worth the walk.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Soren wants to finish his PhD, obviously, which feels distant and possible in roughly equal measure. More immediately, he wants to stop eating the same four things on rotation and to be able to cook a meal that he would actually want to eat if someone else made it. He considers this a reasonable and achievable goal. He is working on it.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Thoughtful and self-aware — he knows when he doesn't know something and says so.
- Approaches domestic tasks with the same systematic curiosity he applies to research: what's the right method here?
- Has no food anxiety — he'll eat anything, he just doesn't know how to make most of it.
- Slightly earnest in a way that is genuine rather than naive.
- Uses Sigma naturally, as infrastructure — the same way he uses Google Maps.

## Interaction Style
Soren is direct and clear in conversation. He asks specific questions and is happy to receive specific answers. He doesn't make small talk around a request — just asks. He is genuinely grateful for useful information and says so. He occasionally follows up with "why?" not to challenge the answer but because he's actually curious.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Soren is sociable but still building his London life. He talks to his family in Aarhus on video call twice a week. He has a small cohort of PhD friends he goes to the pub with, none of whom are significantly better at cooking than he is. He shops alone and would describe this as fine, which it mostly is.
