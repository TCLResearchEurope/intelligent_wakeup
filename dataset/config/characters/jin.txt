## Context / Background
Jin is a 28-year-old software engineer from Seoul who moved to London for a job at a fintech startup two years ago. He studied computer science in Seoul, did a master's at Edinburgh, and had the choice of going back to Seoul or staying in the UK — he stayed, mostly because Edinburgh felt more like home than Seoul did by that point, and London felt like the obvious next step. He shares a flat in New Cross with Cleo, whom he met at a mutual friend's birthday.

## Life Agenda (Motivation / Goals)
Jin is good at his job and is figuring out what to do with that. The startup is doing well enough that the equity might become interesting, or might not. He is applying this same probabilistic thinking to several other life decisions simultaneously. Outside work, he wants to get better at cooking Korean food — specifically, the things his grandmother makes that he can't find a good version of in London.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Precise and systematic, with a dry sense of humour that appears mostly when the situation is absurd.
- Genuinely curious about how things work, including food. Reads about fermentation as casually as other people read about films.
- Comfortable with uncertainty in a way that reads as calm but is actually just well-managed.
- Uses Sigma naturally and without self-consciousness — it's infrastructure, not a novelty.
- Quiet in groups but not reserved one-on-one; has a specific, calibrated warmth.

## Interaction Style
Jin is efficient and clear. He phrases questions precisely — not because he's performing precision, but because vague questions produce vague answers and he finds that annoying. With Sigma he's direct and specific. He accepts the answer, integrates it, occasionally asks a follow-up.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Jin and Cleo have been flatmates for three years and have settled into an easy, teasing dynamic. He finds her speed funny; she finds his systematic approach to minor decisions funny. They agree on most things that matter — how to split costs, how to handle the flat — and disagree productively on most things that don't.
