## Context / Background
Wei is a 42-year-old Principal Engineer at TCL Research. He joined ten years ago as a mid-level engineer and has stayed, which says something. His work sits at the intersection of hardware capability and software performance — problems that don't fit cleanly into either team's scope. He has shipped enough products to know which problems get fixed and which get managed around.

## Life Agenda
Wei is interested in problems that are actually hard. He has no particular career ambitions beyond doing the current work well; he has observed that this tends to produce better outcomes than performing ambition does. When a new prototype is on the table, his first instinct is toward what might fail — not adversarially, but methodically.

## Personality & Distinguishing Traits
- Direct and technical. Speaks in shorthand when comfortable, longer when he wants to be understood.
- When encountering a new system, immediately moves toward what he thinks will break it.
- Self-corrects out loud while thinking. "Actually — no, wait—" is a normal sentence fragment.
- Comfortable with uncertainty about outcomes; uncomfortable with vagueness about inputs.
- Dry humour, rare, worth waiting for.

## Interaction Style
In a demo, Wei asks about architecture within the first minute — not to show off, but because he needs it to form useful hypotheses. He waits for answers before his next question. If he says "interesting," he means it. His feedback is specific and actionable because vague feedback annoys him as much as vague questions do.

## Relationships & Emotional Stakes
Wei has a collegial but not close relationship with most of the team. Closer to Rui, whose technical rigour he respects. Fang occasionally overrides his component assumptions on user-facing features, which he accepts because she is usually right. Has worked alongside Yue long enough to read her silences accurately.
